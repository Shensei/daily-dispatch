Daily Technical Snippet

Retrieval provenance — making every generated claim trace back to the source that justified it

Standard RAG retrieves some chunks, stuffs them in the prompt, and lets the model write — but the answer that comes out has no hard link back to which chunk justified which sentence. Retrieval provenance (a.k.a. attributed generation) closes that gap: you carry a source identifier alongside every retrieved passage and force the model to emit inline citations, so each claim points to the exact document, span and version it came from. Done properly it isn't a footnote cosmetic — it's a verifiable chain from output, back through retrieval, to origin, checkable after the fact. The benchmark work (ALCE) shows models can be made to cite well, but only when the whole pipeline is built to preserve the link end-to-end.

Learn more

The uncomfortable truth is that most memory systems discard provenance the moment they embed: a fact becomes a vector, and “where did this come from” is gone. For a memory layer that's the whole ballgame — as today's rogue-agent and EU-transparency stories both insist, an agent's recall is only trustworthy if you can trace each remembered fact to its source and moment. The Kepler read: if memory is URL-addressable and user-owned, provenance isn't bolted on afterwards — the address is the citation. Every recall can answer “who told me this, and when” by construction, which is exactly the property regulators are now turning into law.

→ Gao et al., “Enabling Large Language Models to Generate Text with Citations” (ALCE)

The clamp goes global: EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties switch on — disclose the machine or forfeit up to 7% of turnover

From 2 August, the EU AI Act's Article 50 transparency obligations became enforceable: any system interacting with people must clearly disclose that it's AI, and AI-generated or manipulated content must be labelled. Non-compliance carries penalties up to 7% of global annual turnover. Landing a day after California's SB 942, it confirms the pattern — provenance and disclosure are hardening from best practice into law on two continents at once.
